Key findings

  • A three-step response (quiet change → offer → human) kept unwanted prompts rare.
  • Playtests exposed real layout problems (a hatch hidden by a task spot, a lost quest marker) that we fixed.
  • Crew trust depends on consent first: every check-in asks before acting.

Future improvements

  • MindRing prototype: bench-test PPG and HRV against a chest strap, then a stress-task study.
  • More LUNA-X wearables: AR glasses, watch, audio companion and a habitat wellness wall.
  • Real NASA use: pilot in an analog habitat (HERA-style crew study) before any flight use.

Results come from our own simulations and playtests, not from human trials. MindRing figures are design targets.

Problem statement

Long Missions Challenge More Than the Body

Isolation, poor sleep, cramped rooms and the same faces every day wear crews down. NASA's Human Research Program counts behavioral health among the key risks of long missions, and crews often under-report strain. On the Moon, a missed early warning costs sleep, performance and safety.

Mission day 147: MindRing flags three short nights and falling heart-rate variability before the crew member says a word.

Project goals
  • Detect early. Spot stress, fatigue, low mood and isolation weeks before they become a crisis.
  • Respond gently. Let the habitat adapt (light, sound, greenhouse time) before asking anything of the crew.
  • Protect privacy. Health data stays onboard; a "no" always stops the AI; humans decide.
  • Prove it. Test every idea in models, simulations and playable prototypes.

Expected outcomes

Fewer sleepless nights, earlier support, and a crew that trusts the system because it asks first.

A shared core to live in, a private space that is truly yours, and help you are free to turn down.
Key features

One Habitat That Looks After Its Crew

1 · Artemis Hub habitat

A shielded central dome, a ring corridor of four districts and four private sleep pods inside a regolith berm at Shackleton crater.

Blueprint: dome, ring districts (living, farm, water, shelter), pods, berm and gates.

2 · LUNA crew-health AI

Reads sleep, heart rate and how often people meet; responds in three steps: a quiet change, an offer, then a human.

LUNA's crew wellness board: one score per person, data stays onboard.

3 · MindRing wellness ring

PPG, on-demand ECG, skin conductance and temperature, with AI on the ring itself. It asks "Is this accurate?" before acting.

MindRing sensor array: three PPG paths, skin-conductance and temperature contacts.

4 · Adaptive environment

When stress rises, lighting warms, a soundscape starts and the greenhouse opens. The crew always has the final say.

Stress spike detected; the habitat answers with light, sound and greenhouse time.
