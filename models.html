01 · The habitat

Built to keep the Moon out

The Moon has no air, deadly radiation, ±150 °C swings, micrometeoroids and razor-sharp dust. The realistic habitat stops each of these with its own layer, from the outside in:

1Regolith shield domes

3D-printed from Moon soil and piled over every module. Blocks most galactic radiation and stops micrometeoroids before they reach the hull.

2Multi-layer insulation

Gold MLI blankets around each hull keep the inside at about 21 °C through the 14-day lunar night.

3Pressure hull

Rigid aluminium lower deck plus an inflatable upper deck hold 70.3 kPa of breathable air.

4Hatches between modules

Every tunnel ends in bulkhead hatches, so a leak in one module can be sealed off while the crew moves to safety.

5Dust lock + suitports

Crew climb into suits docked to the wall, so lunar dust never comes inside. Air knives clean anything that does.

6Storm shelter

The medical node doubles as a shielded shelter for solar storms, with jump seats for all four crew.

02 · Health monitoring · designed in Blender

The hardware that watches over the crew

Our AI looks after crew mental and physical health using nine sensors (deliverable D1). These are the devices that carry them. Every one is small, light, low-power, and keeps the raw data on the device: only a few numbers leave it.

1 · SenseBeacon, wristband, patch and zone pods take readings.
→
2 · ScoreReadings are compared with each person's own 14-day baseline.
→
3 · DecideSix condition scores, three tiers. No single sensor can trigger an action.
→
4 · HelpLight, sound and schedule nudges first. A human doctor makes Tier-3 decisions.
