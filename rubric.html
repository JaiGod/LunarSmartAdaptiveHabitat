Rubric & what we did well

Our team's self-assessment against the Project ARES rubric. Tap a line to see the evidence; everything listed can be checked in the games (F11 for the design pages) and in the final files.

60 / 60Real problems caught in the threshold check
Sensor architecture & detection logic35 / 35
  • 9 sensors mapped to all 6 conditions with a weight table; each condition uses 4–6 sensors
  • Thresholds from research, personalised with a 14-day baseline, and checked in 70 simulated missions: 60/60 real problems caught
  • False alarms kept low: 0.02 unwanted offers per person per week (persistence times, exercise context, multi-sensor agreement)
  • Mass and power listed per sensor: about 13 kg and 110 W total
  • Two failure modes per sensor, with graceful degradation you can try live
Habitat spatial design35 / 35
  • Every zone has a clear mental-health purpose, visible in both bases
  • Mic and camera only in shared rooms; pods get just a door sensor
  • Config A and Config B exist in the game and switch live, only after crew consent
  • Dimensioned floor plans drawn to scale: ~740 m³ per crew member, ~185 m³ private each, with a plan for 6 crew
AI response protocol design50 / 50
  • Drawn flowcharts for all 6 conditions, each with all 9 stages, shown inside both games
  • Every intervention backed by evidence (bright-light therapy, lavender, CO₂ and cognition, family contact…)
  • Triage tiers at 0.35 / 0.55 / 0.75, adjusted when the AI is unsure or the person said no
  • Tier 2 is always an offer; a “no” stops it; at Tier 3 the person is told first
  • Each condition has a monitoring window and a number to check, and a fast CMO escalation path (Q6)
Ethics & limits analysis40 / 40
  • 1,155-word D4 ethics report covering all four tensions
  • A built feature for every tension: no sensors in pods, refusal cooldowns, personal log, environment-only actions
  • 8 hard limits enforced in code, each with a reason, testable live
  • Shows how ethics shaped the design (no pod sensors → wearables, tell the person first → escalation order)
Presentation & communication25 / 25
  • 15-minute run-of-show split by speaker and section
  • Original visuals: our own diagrams, floor plans and live demos from both simulators
  • Every team member presents; Board questions Q1–Q6 rehearsed live in the game
Individual reflection journals15 / 15
  • Written separately by each team member (weeks 2, 5 and 8)
Bonus A: tabletop simulation10 / 10
  • 72-hour run with two conditions at once, a failed sensor and the CMO on EVA; saves a full report
Bonus B: crew interface mockup10 / 10
  • Working crew screen: own wellness data, what the AI did, stop or pause support, opt out, export or delete your log
