LUNA TRAINING HUB

Building the intelligence behind the habitat

Luna is the assistant that runs our lunar habitat. She does not arrive knowing everything — she gets better because the crew corrects her. This page is where that happens. Ask her something, tell her when she is wrong, show her a photograph, run her through an emergency. Every one of those changes what she knows.

How she learns

One cycle, six steps, every time you rate an answer or score a drill. The steps light up as they actually run, so this is not a diagram of the process — it is the process.

Teach her

Luna answers from a knowledge file of short, plain sentences — astronauts reading something at three in the morning do not want a paragraph. If an answer is good, say so and she will lean on it more. If it is wrong, type the right one and she will use yours instead.

Most trusted answers

Try something she has no answer for, like where is the spare helmet, then teach her and ask again.

Show her a photograph

Drop in a picture of a panel, a rover, a piece of equipment. Luna measures brightness, colour spread and edge detail in your browser and runs rule-based checks on them. It is a demonstration of the idea, not a trained vision model, and the picture never leaves your device.

What comes next

Five stages. A stage is marked done when this page can actually demonstrate it, not when it sounds good on a slide.

What this page is. A simulation written in plain JavaScript. Luna's answers come from a knowledge file I wrote, matched with keyword rules — there is no large language model behind it and nothing is sent anywhere. The learning is real in the sense that your ratings genuinely change the weights and the stored answers, which is the behaviour the design is meant to show.
